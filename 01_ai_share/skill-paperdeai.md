# Paper De-AI Skill

## Core Principle

De-AI ≠ dumbing down. The goal is to make the text read like it was written by a thinking human scholar, not to lower the quality.

- **Preserve**: core arguments, data, logical structure, technical terminology, citation format
- **Eliminate**: the "template feel", "symmetry obsession", "over-polished perfection", "correct but empty filler"
- **Inject**: leaps of thought, voice and personality, varied pacing, occasional "imperfection"

---

## AI Writing Signature Checklist

Scan the text first. The more boxes it ticks, the more it needs rewriting:

### Sentence Level
- Heavy use of mechanical parallelism: "first... second... finally...", "on one hand... on the other hand..."
- Every paragraph is nearly the same length; paragraph structure is highly symmetrical
- Sentence length is uniform; no alternation between long and short sentences
- Frequent transitional filler: "in conclusion", "it is worth noting that", "as can be seen"
- Every paragraph follows the perfect "topic sentence + development + summary" formula

### Vocabulary Level
- Overuse of vague grand words: "significant", "crucial", "indispensable", "profound"
- Adjective stacking: "comprehensively, systematically, and deeply" appears in triplets
- Verb preference: corporate/official-sounding verbs ("facilitate", "enhance", "leverage", "foster")
- Excessive nominalization: using noun phrases where a verb would do ("conduct an analysis of" instead of "analyze")

### Content Level
- Every argument has exactly three sub-points ("triplet syndrome")
- Both sides of every issue are covered, but no clear stance is taken
- Conclusions always hedge: "we must balance A and B", "both X and Y are important"
- Definition-style openings everywhere: "X refers to..."
- Lacks specific, personal judgment; everything is "safe and correct"

### Logic Level
- Transitions are too smooth; no thought discontinuities
- Every conclusion has perfectly supporting evidence
- No "this question remains to be further verified" moments
- Reads like reciting an outline, not thinking through an idea

## De-AI Workflow

Follow these four steps in order. Don't skip.

### Step 1: Read Through and Mark
Read the whole text once. Annotate:
- Which paragraphs have obvious "AI flavor" (filler, parallelism, vague words)
- Which are core arguments/data/logic (must preserve)
- Which are "correct but empty filler" (can be deleted or heavily compressed)

### Step 2: Cut Aggressively
Subtract first, then add:
1. Delete all transition sentences that carry no new information ("in summary", "as mentioned above", etc.)
2. Turn three-part parallel structures into two or one — break the symmetry
3. Compress definition-style openings; get straight to the point
4. Delete "on one hand... on the other hand..." fence-sitting conclusions; take a clear position

### Step 3: Rewrite Sentence by Sentence
Apply these techniques paragraph by paragraph:

#### Technique 1: Mix Long and Short Sentences
Turn uniformly sized sentences into a rhythm of varied lengths.
- AI style: "This study employed a mixed-methods approach combining quantitative and qualitative research, comprehensively analyzed the characteristics of the data, and systematically explored the relationships between variables."
- Rewritten: "This study used mixed methods. Quantitative analysis mapped the data first, then qualitative interviews dug into the mechanisms behind the variables."

#### Technique 2: Remove Adverb Stacking
Words like "comprehensively, systematically, deeply" can almost always be deleted, or replaced with something specific.
- AI style: "deeply explored multiple dimensions of the problem"
- Rewritten: "focused on dimensions X and Y" (naming the dimensions is more concrete than "deeply")

#### Technique 3: Replace Nominalization with Verbs
- AI style: "conducted a comprehensive data analysis"
- Rewritten: "analyzed all the data"

#### Technique 4: Break the Triplets
Three parallel arguments/methods/causes → make it two, or keep three but give them different weight and space.
- AI style: "There are three influencing factors: economic, social, and cultural." (each gets a paragraph of equal length)
- Rewritten: "Among the influencing factors, economic factors have the most direct effect (detailed discussion); social factors are secondary (brief explanation); cultural factors play a more indirect role (one sentence)."

#### Technique 5: Inject Personal Judgment
Add statements with the author's stance. Give the writing a "human" feel.
- AI style: "This result has important theoretical significance and practical value."
- Rewritten: "This result at least suggests that the X effect, widely assumed in prior literature, may have been overestimated — at least in our sample."

#### Technique 6: Add Traces of Thinking
Keep some "thought process" visible — note limitations, acknowledge contradictions, raise questions. Make it feel less like a canned answer.
- AI style: "Our results are consistent with existing research and confirm the correctness of Theory X."
- Rewritten: "Our results broadly support Theory X, but we see a deviation in context Z — whether this is a sample bias or a boundary effect of the theory is hard to say for now, and needs further investigation."

#### Technique 7: Replace Vague Grand Words
Swap "significant", "important", "key", "profound" for specific descriptions.
- AI style: "had a significant impact"
- Rewritten: "increased Y by approximately 30%" (use data when available) / "the impact was most visible in dimension X" (specify where, when no data)

## Section-Specific Tips

### Abstract
- Common AI problem: background + methods + results + conclusion, four neatly equal segments, sentences of similar length
- Fix:
  - Make the background sentence shorter, or cut straight in
  - Let results speak with data, fewer "significantly"s
  - Don't make the conclusion too "perfect" — leave a hint of openness
  - Break the "one sentence = one information unit" uniformity

### Introduction
- Common AI problem: literature review reads like a checklist — one sentence per paper, highly symmetrical
- Fix:
  - Give papers different amounts of space; important ones get more, minor ones get a passing mention
  - Add the author's evaluation and choices, not neutral listing
  - State the research gap specifically, not vague "insufficient research" / "needs further study"
  - Add a sentence or two of thought trace: "We initially expected... but found..."

### Methodology
- Common AI problem: step-by-step description is too neat; each subsection has the same length
- Fix:
  - Detail the core method, skim the auxiliary ones — create a gap in depth
  - Explain why you chose this method (with judgment, not objective statement)
  - Mention limitations or trade-offs ("due to..., we used... rather than...")

### Results
- Common AI problem: every figure/table gets a paragraph of equal length; format is highly uniform
- Fix:
  - Describe important results in detail and interpret them; merge minor ones
  - Don't start every paragraph with "As shown in Figure X..." — vary the openings
  - Mention unexpected results ("Notably, X deviated from expectations...")

### Discussion
- Common AI problem: covers everything, sees both sides, takes no stance; structure is too symmetrical
- Fix:
  - Discuss selectively; don't give every result equal airtime
  - Offer clear explanations and judgments; no fence-sitting "it could be A, it could be B"
  - Acknowledge uncertainty in interpretation, but lean toward a position
  - Break the mechanical "consistent/inconsistent with prior studies" comparison framework

### Conclusion
- Common AI problem: summarizes the paper + looks to the future, always positive, always "both X and Y"
- Fix:
  - Don't just restate the abstract — add a synthesis or new judgment
  - Make the outlook specific, not empty "has broad application prospects"
  - Be more honest about limitations; don't tuck them into one throwaway sentence at the end

## Quality Self-Check Checklist

After rewriting, verify item by item:

- [ ] Do paragraphs vary in length? (they shouldn't all be similar)
- [ ] Do sentences alternate between long and short? (not all long, not all short)
- [ ] Are there fewer than 2 instances of "first/second/finally" or "on one hand/on the other hand"?
- [ ] Have vague grand words ("significant", "important", "key", "profound") been replaced or removed?
- [ ] Are there still "triplet" three-part parallel structures? (if yes, fix them)
- [ ] Is there at least 2 places with the author's personal judgment?
- [ ] Is there at least 1 place acknowledging limitation/uncertainty/opening?
- [ ] Are transitions still too smooth? (a little "roughness" feels more human)
- [ ] Are core arguments, data, and logic fully preserved?
- [ ] Are technical terms and citation format untouched?

---

## Notes

1. **Don't overdo it**: de-AI means making it read like a serious scholar wrote it, not making it messy.
2. **Keep professionalism**: technical terms, formulas, data, and citation format stay untouched — only the expression changes.
3. **Respect the original meaning**: core ideas and argumentation logic don't change — you're repackaging, not rewriting the kernel.
4. **Handle section by section**: AI flavor shows differently in different sections; use the section-specific tips above.
5. **Chinese papers**: same principles apply — AI Chinese is characterized by excessive parallelism, nominalization, official-sounding verbs, and symmetric paragraph structures.
